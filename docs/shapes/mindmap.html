Mind Map Shapes

Bubbles, rectangles, circles, lines and connectors for laying out ideas on the board. Connectors stay attached to the shapes they link, so the diagram keeps its meaning while you rearrange it.

Screenshot: mind map on the board

Overview

The mind map set is grouped under a single dropdown in the main toolbar. Pick a shape, then drag on the board to place it: nodes are drawn as a rectangle, connectors as a segment from one point to another. Double-click a node to type its text.

Unlike the Line, Vector and Arc shapes, mind map shapes are annotations rather than simulation objects. They are not bound to calculator terms and do not need a referential, so they can be placed anywhere on the board.

The shapes

ShapeDescription
BubbleRounded speech bubble with a tail, for comments and asides
RectangleRounded box, the workhorse node for a topic or step
CircleEllipse, typically the central idea of the map
LineA plain segment with no tips, for grouping or underlining
ConnectorAn arrow that links two shapes and follows them when they move

Connecting shapes

Shapes have no fixed connection points. Draw a connector — or drag the end of an existing one — and drop it anywhere inside another shape, including charts, tables, expressions and images: that exact spot becomes the attachment point. Dropping on empty canvas leaves that end free instead.

While you drag an end over a shape, that shape is highlighted so you can see it will become the target; move off it or over empty canvas and the highlight disappears. An end can't attach to the shape the connector's other end is already attached to.

An attached end is not a fixed coordinate: it is stored as a position relative to the shape's own box — for example "a quarter of the way across, a quarter of the way down" — and recomputed from the shape's current size, position and rotation on every redraw. Resize the shape and the point slides to keep the same relative spot; move or rotate it and the connector follows. Deleting a shape also deletes the connectors attached to it, and a single undo brings back both.

Select a connector to drag either endpoint. Dropping an endpoint on a different shape re-attaches it there, at the new drop point; dropping it on empty canvas detaches it.

Node properties

PropertyDescription
TextLabel shown inside the shape; double-click the shape to edit it
Font sizeSize of the label text
Text colorColour of the label text
Border widthThickness of the outline
Fill and border colorSet from the appearance dropdown, together with opacity, which fades the fill and leaves the border and the name alone

Connector properties

PropertyDescription
Start tip / End tipNone, arrow, filled arrow, dot or diamond at each end
TypeStraight, curved or orthogonal path between the endpoints
Line styleSolid or dashed
Line widthThickness of the connector
LabelText placed on the connector, with a slider to move it along the line

Select a connector to reveal a third handle on its path, in addition to the two endpoint handles. Dragging it reshapes the connector: for a straight connector it kinks the line into two segments meeting at an angle; for a curved connector it adjusts the curve, on top of the gentle bulge it already has by default; for an orthogonal connector it slides the elbow along whichever axis the path turns on.

Double-click a connector to type its label right on the line, the same way double-clicking a node edits its text. Once there is a label, drag it to slide it along the connector — the same effect as the Label Position slider in the toolbar, but directly on the canvas.
